WIWANT

Launched: October 2021

An app to find your perfect wine.

WIWANT, an experienced wine accessories company, aims to shift from selling accessories to wine itself due to competitive pressures. To support this transition, they sought an app to track users' wine preferences, partnering with our Taiwan-based software consulting firm. In this project, I designed the app from the ground up and collaborated solely with a marketing manager and engineers.

Problem

All wine lovers have experienced buying a wine they didn't enjoy. Amateur wine lovers struggle to understand their tastes, while professionals struggle to connect with other enthusiasts.

Wine discovery is often driven by generic ratings that don't reflect personal taste. Amateur wine lovers struggle to understand what they like, while professional wine lovers have limited opportunities to connect and share their knowledge. For WIWANT, the goal is to drive wine accessory purchases while building toward a future as a wine retailer.

Challenge

How did I start from scratch with no resources?

Competitor Research

Other Wine App Users' Main Complaint: Taste Doesn't Match Ratings or Reviews from Apps and Fellow Wine Lovers

Based on suggestions from forums and Apple stores, I selected four competitors popular among wine lovers for analysis. My aim is to understand their target users, business goals, features, user experience, and potential growth areas. The main complaint I found among users is dissatisfaction with recommended wines, such as their ratings and reviews.

Quantitative and Qualitative Research

All wine lovers experience drinking wine that does not meet their tastes, but 74% are still open to trying new wine. What makes them want to try?

To explore more about how wine lovers choose the wine they want to buy and the reasons that motivate wine lovers to try new wines despite potential disappointment, I joined wine lovers' groups, surveyed 27 participants, and conducted interviews with 6. The main themes are outlined below.

How long have you been drinking wine?

How often do you drink wine and on what occasions?

What made you decide to start drinking wine?

How do you decide which wine to buy? Any tools you use?

Have you ever chosen wine that didn't match your taste? How do you avoid this?

Have you ever use any other apps? Why do you use it and any pros and cons?

Persona

Let's meet Alan the amateur wine lover, Paul the professional wine lover, and Wendy the WIWANT's founder.

  • Alan

    Amateur Wine Lover

    “ I often drink wines that disappoint me. I'm unsure if it's due to my amateur status or simply because they are not to my taste. ”

    Goals:

    • Embrace feedback to continue learning.
    • Discover wines that suit my taste.
    • Share the joy of wine with peers.

    Frustrations:

    • Uncertain about whether the wine meets his expectations.
    • Unreliable rating on other apps.
    • Unsure if he is drinking the wine properly.

  • Paul

    Professional Wine Lover

    “ I am a restaurant owner who offers wine pairings and enjoys sharing my knowledge with others. I hope to share more with fellow enthusiasts and make new friends. ”

    Goals:

    • Discuss with other experts.
    • Share my experiences with others.
    • Discover new wines for my food pairings.

    Motivations:

    • Enjoy hearing customers' feedback on my pairings.
    • Exchange thoughts with wine experts.
    • Explore new wines.

  • Wendy

    WIWANT's Founder

    “ Due to low sales of wine accessories, we aim to enter the $800 million wine market. An app could help us gather user preferences for wine purchases and promote our accessories. ”

    Goals:

    • Expand the market to include wine sales alongside wine accessories.
    • Attract more wine lovers to gather wine preference data and to promote wine accessories.

    Frustrations:

    • Sales of wine accessories are low.
    • We do not know which type of wine to purchase first to sell.

Main Iterations

How Did I Get from the Initial Concept to the Final Product?
Balancing User Needs, Technical Feasibility, Business Goals, and Project Scope

Before

Feasibility Feedback - Various Prices

Before selling wine, WIWANT did not consider providing the price of wine, as wine prices vary by stores due to imported taxes.

After

User Feedback - Wine Pricing

Users want to get a sense of how much a wine costs. We'll start with USD from other retailers, then switch to the TWD average after collecting enough purchase prices from users.

Before

Design Choice - Setting Rates Apart

Originally, I decided to use percentages for the match rate to distinguish it from the market rate.

After

Feasibility Feedback - Calculation

However, we discovered that the calculation was complex, and found it challenging to users to navigate the wide range. Achieving a perfect score of 100 was difficult. Therefore, we opted to set the maximum score to 5 instead.

Before

Design Choice - Direct browsing

Originally, when designing my notes page, I only included tabs for users to select the information they were seeking.

After

User Feedback - Filter

After usability tests, users expressed a need to quickly find wines they've enjoyed before when socializing. Therefore, I added filter options for this purpose.

Reflection

Engage Your Stakeholders, Don't Just Tell Them

I often found myself acting as the intermediary between clients and engineers. In the first few projects, I struggled to convince clients of the value of UX due to a limited number of interviewees caused by budget constraints. However, after gaining some experience, I realized that engaging stakeholders at every stage of the project makes a significant difference. They become more receptive and are able to put themselves in the users' shoes, providing valuable feedback.
