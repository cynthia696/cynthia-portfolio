Permissions

Launched: March 2025

Flexible, adaptable permissions for any user and team

At Gripp, we work with clients of all sizes, from small teams of around 10 people to large organizations with over 500 employees. With so many staff members joining and leaving, some clients began requesting more control over what their employees can see. This led to the need for a permissions feature with different access levels to help them manage visibility across their teams.

Context

How did the previous invitation process work?

In the old design, tapping the orange '+' button instantly revealed an invitation code. Anyone could scan and join, gaining full access to all activities, groups, and assets. It felt like a new employee seeing everything from finance to HR at once. For the company, this exposed too much, and for employees, it wasn’t clear where to focus.

Problem

Temporary workers and visitors often join farms for short-term tasks or specific equipment, but they can still see everything happening on the farm. Farmers need a way to limit access so people only see what's relevant.

I manage teams and employees, but right now everyone has full access, and it’s hard to control.

Challenge

How do I design a permission system that accommodates different client requests and user personas while ensuring seamless updates across pages?

Ideation

After understanding the needs of different companies and personas, I reviewed our current flows and the key pages affected.

With a clear understanding of the personas and business goals, I explored solution concepts aligned to user needs. After defining the ideal solution, I created task flows for each persona and evaluated the key pages affected across the product.

Main Iterations

How Did I Get from the Initial Concept to the Final Product?
Balancing User Needs, Technical Feasibility, Business Goals, and Project Scope

Before

Design Choice - Notification

To help temporary workers stay on top of tasks across different workplaces, I designed notifications that alert them whenever they are mentioned in any workplace.

After

Teams Feedback – All Activity

After discussing with the team, we decided to include an All Activity option, which not only lets users stay on top of multiple workplaces but also view their past completed tasks.

Before

Design Choice - Permission Flexibility

Originally, I designed the system so that each company has the flexibility to set different access levels, as each company has different needs.

After

Feasibility Test - Fixed Permissions

However, considering the timeline and scope, we decided that for the MVP, permissions will be fixed for all companies, since giving flexibility to set them would require extra time.

Before

Design Choice - User-Friendly Names

When designing the names for different permission account types, I chose roles like Manager, Worker, and Viewer, as these are more user-friendly for clients.

After

Design Choice - Content Design

When reviewing roles with different clients, I noticed that not every client uses the same names like Manager, Worker, and Viewer. Even when they do, the permissions for these roles often differ.

Reflection

Business v.s. UX Tradeoff

I often found myself acting as the intermediary between clients and engineers. In the first few projects, I struggled to convince clients of the value of UX due to a limited number of interviewees caused by budget constraints. However, after gaining some experience, I realized that engaging stakeholders at every stage of the project makes a significant difference. They become more receptive and are able to put themselves in the users' shoes, providing valuable feedback.
